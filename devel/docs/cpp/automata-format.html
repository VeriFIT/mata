The Mata file format for automata (.mata format)

Warning
The Mata library currently supports only a subset of the whole format. Notably, the Mata library supports NFAs and NFTs (transducers), but not AFAs (their support is disabled). The Mata library may also not support all the additional features described in the format, such as special formulas, tracks, etc.

Top-level file structure

  • The format is line-based. Lines can be connected by \.
  • Lines are parsed into tokens. Tokens are delimited by white spaces, the format is white space sensitive with exception of formulas in transitions where expressions could be written without white spaces separating tokens.
  • A file contains sections starting with a line of the form @<SECTION-TYPE>, containing an automaton or, in theory, anything. We will start with one section per file, containing an automaton, but it is obviously extensible to more sections and more kinds of things.
  • In automata sections, non-empty lines are key-value lines of the form %<KEY> [VALUE], transition lines, or comment lines starting with #. Several key-value lines with the same key mean that the key is mapped to the set of values, an occurrence without a value marks that the KEY is defined.
  • Besides white spaces and the end of line, the following are characters with special meaning: &,|,!,@,(,),%,",\,# [,],a,q,n,t,f.
    • &,|,!,(,) are logical operators used in Boolean formulas.
    • [,],-,^ enclose character classes, such as [abcd-h], [^abcd-h].
    • @ opens the line with a section name and is used in transducer alphabet tokens of the form x@a1, y@[10-55],...
    • % opens a key-value line.
    • " strings containing white spaces and special characters can be written in between ". The special characters lose their special meaning. The characters " and \ inside a string must be escaped, i.e. \"</tt> and <tt>\\\\</tt>. - <tt>\\</tt> for escaping characters. <tt>\\</tt> is also used to concatenate lines. - <tt>a</tt>,<tt>q</tt>,<tt>n</tt>,<tt>t</tt>,<tt>f</tt> are token type specifiers (alphabet, state, node, attribute, formula). - <tt>\#</tt> starts a comment line. - There are <strong>words with a special meaning</strong>: <tt>\\true</tt>,<tt>\\false</tt>, <tt>\\min</tt>, <tt>\\max</tt>. The first two represent true/false in Boolean formulas. - The parser may recognise the special symbols as standalone tokens (does not apply to <tt>q</tt>,<tt>a</tt>,<tt>n</tt>,<tt>t</tt>,<tt>f</tt>,<tt>\#</tt>) only if they appear in the special context where their special meaning applies, otherwise they should be treated as normal symbols. We categorise automata according to their <strong>transition type</strong>, that is, the structure of their transitions, and by their <strong>alphabet type</strong>, i.e., how alphabet symbols on transitions are represented. It is expected that different parameters would be parsed into different data structures. These parameters should determine the name of the automata section. @section alphabet-type Alphabet type We categorise automata by the <strong>alphabet type</strong>, i.e., the representation of symbols on transitions. We will consider propositional formulae (over bit vectors), explicit symbols, intervals of numbers or unicode or ascii, or user-defined alphabet. - <strong>Explicit</strong>: Just plain symbols. May be given implicitly or enumerated, or one can have some predefined alphabet, numbers, ascii, utf. - <strong>Bitvector</strong>: Propositional variables, the syntax is the same as for explicit symbols. - <strong>Character class</strong>: Character classes, i.e. sets of symbols as used in normal regular expressions, are of the form <tt>[bla]</tt> or <tt>[^bla]</tt> where <tt>bla</tt> is a sequence of symbols and intervals of symbols such as <tt>a-z</tt>, for instance <tt>abcd-hij-z</tt> denotes all lower case alphabet symbols. The <tt>^</tt> in front complements the entire class. The character <tt>-</tt> in sincluded as <tt>\\-</tt>, analogously <tt>\\</tt> and <tt>^</tt>. Note that a single explicit symbol or a single interval is also a transition formula. - Both symbol literals and states are determined positionally. Markers or enumeration can also be used. - Initial (final) states are defined by key-value line <tt>%Initial\"</tt> (<tt>%Final</tt>) followed by an enumeration of states. @subsection afa-alternating-finite-automata AFA (Alternating finite automata) \warning The Mata library currently does not support AFAs; their support is disabled. - Transition lines are of the form <tt>\"\<State\> \<Formula\>\"</tt> representing the source state and the transition formula - a Boolean formula over symbol literals, states, and nodes. - Several lines starting with the same state mean the disjunciton of the formulae. No line for the state means false. - Initial (final) states are defined by key-value line <tt>%Initial \<Formula\>\"</tt> (<tt>\"%Final \<Formula\>\"</tt>) where the Boolean formula is given over states. We therefore require one of the three schemes for distinguishing token types: - By <strong>enumeration</strong>. - By <strong>type-markers</strong> (default). The literal is preceeded by a single character that specifies the type, we use <tt>q</tt> for states, <tt>a</tt> for symbols, and <tt>n</tt> for shared nodes. The marker <em>is</em> a part of the thing's name. - <strong>Automatic</strong>. If all the other types are specified by enumeration or by markers, the third type may be marked as automatic, the parser will assign it to everything which is not of the two previous types. - The typing scheme is specified as a key value line with the key <tt>\<Thing\>-\<Scheme\></tt> where <tt>\<Thing\></tt> may be <tt>Alphabet</tt>, <tt>States</tt>, or <tt>Nodes</tt>. <tt>\<Scheme\></tt> may be <tt>marked</tt>, <tt>enum</tt>, or <tt>auto</tt>. The scheme <tt>enum</tt> is followed by an enumeration. For instance, we can have <tt>%Alphabet-enum a b c karel</tt>, <tt>%Alphabet-auto</tt>, <tt>%Alphabet-marked</tt>. - Moreverover, in case of alphabet one can also use <tt>utf-8</tt>, <tt>chars</tt>, or <tt>numbers</tt> as typing scheme to denote that an alphabet is all utf-8 symbols, all characters, or all numbers respectively. - Shared nodes are used to share same formula or part of formula among more transitions. We support specifying <strong>epsilons</strong>, by a key-value line <tt>%Epsilon \<formula\></tt> where formula has the same syntax and meaning as the formulae used in transitions (depends on the alphabet type and typing scheme). The formula may be just a single letter which is supposed to mean epsilon, it may be a disjunciton of letters, when we need several different epsilons, or a more complex formula describing boolean vectors that are supposed to mean epsilons. The attributes are meant as something not relevant for the semantics of the automaton, for instance some attributes used when siplaying the automaton, such as colors. 1. An attribute inside a transition is a token such as a state, node, symbol. Its identifier is <tt>t</tt>. We can also give attributes to states, symbols, nodes by writing key-value lines of the form <tt>%Attribute \<state\> \<attribute\></tt>. @section how-the-mata-library-reads-the-format How the Mata library reads the format The loaders of the Mata library (<tt>mata::nfa::builder::parse_from_mata()</tt>, <tt>mata::nft::builder::parse_from_mata()</tt>, and the loaders of the Python and Rust bindings) give each section to a fast reader when the section stays within the subset the reader covers, and to the general reader otherwise. Both read the same automaton, up to the numbering of states and of the minterms of bitvector alphabets. The general reader parses the section with <tt>mata::parser::parse_mf()</tt>. For NFAs, <tt>mata::IntermediateAut</tt> then parses the formulas of the section and <tt>construct()</tt> builds the automaton from it. For NFTs, <tt>construct()</tt> builds the transducer from the parsed section directly, as its <tt>mata::IntermediateAut</tt> overload ignores <tt>%Levels</tt> and <tt>%LevelsNum</tt>. - <strong>Explicit reader</strong> (<tt>mata::nfa::builder::parse_explicit_from_mata()</tt>, and its counterpart for NFTs): a single <tt>\@NFA</tt> or <tt>\@NFA-explicit</tt> section (<tt>\@NFT</tt> or <tt>\@NFT-explicit</tt> for NFTs) in which - the <tt>%Initial</tt> and <tt>%Final</tt> lines (and <tt>%Levels</tt> and <tt>%LevelsNum</tt> for NFTs) are plain lists of names, - every transition is a <tt>source symbol target</tt> triple of names, - the namings of states, symbols, and nodes are <tt>auto</tt> or <tt>marked</tt>, - and no unquoted name contains <tt>\&</tt>, <tt>|</tt>, <tt>!</tt>, <tt>(</tt>, <tt>)</tt>, <tt>%</tt>, <tt>\@</tt>, or <tt>\".

  • Bitvector reader (mata::nfa::builder::BitvectorNfaReader): @NFA-bits sections, all of which given to one reader share one set of minterms. It reads directly the sections in which

    • every transition is source formula target, where the formula is a Boolean combination of unquoted symbol names, \true, and \false with !, &, |, and parentheses, not joined to the target by &,
    • Initial is a plain list of states,
    • Final is a plain list of states, a conjunction of negated states such as !q1 & !q2, \true, or \false,
    • and the namings are auto or marked.

    The other @NFA-bits sections, such as those with states in transition formulas, a disjunction in Initial, or quoted names, are read by the general reader inside the bitvector reader, still over the shared minterms.

  • General reader: everything else, such as other section types, Boolean formulas in explicit sections, enumerated namings (States-enum, Alphabet-enum), and malformed inputs, which it reports.
