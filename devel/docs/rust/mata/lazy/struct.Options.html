pub fn max_states(self, max_states: usize) -> Options

Fails with StateLimit once the query holds more than max_states macrostates, so a query that answers holds at most max_states. The successors of a macrostate are counted step by step, a step being a move of a product’s left operand or a tuple of a complement. A query stops after the step that passes the limit, and a step of a product can create one macrostate per matching move of the right operand.

Source

pub fn time_limit(self, limit: Duration) -> Options

Fails with TimeLimit once the query runs longer than limit. The clock is read every 256 steps of the search and of the loops that create macrostates, during a leaf’s simulation, and every few thousand comparisons of a minimization, so a zero limit still lets a small query answer. A step runs to its end first, such as a move of a product’s left operand joined with many moves of the right one.

A simulation cut short by the limit is not kept, so a query whose limit is shorter than a leaf’s simulation fails every time. Give one query enough time (or no limit), and later queries of the formula reuse its simulation. Queries on other threads wait, up to their own limit, for a simulation that one of them computes.

Source

pub fn subsumption(self, enabled: bool) -> Options

Prunes with simulation-based subsumption (on by default). Complement sets drop the members below another member, and the search keeps the antichain of antichain. Off, the search is a plain reachability check, which helps to cross-check results.

Source

pub fn antichain(self, enabled: bool) -> Options

Skips the macrostates of the checked term that are below one found before (on by default; only with subsumption). Each new macrostate is compared with those found, which can cost more than it saves, for example on a product of deterministic automata. Off, complement sets are still minimized.

Source

pub fn cache_budget(self, bytes: usize) -> Options

Keeps the successors that the query caches within bytes (by default it keeps all). Over the budget, it drops those that took the least time to compute per byte, weighted by their reads, and were not read again since (GreedyDual-Size with frequency). It computes a dropped successor again when it needs it, so only the time, dropped and the posts of the nodes change, not the answer or the other statistics.

Source

pub fn plan(self, plan: Plan) -> Options

Annotates the nodes of the query with plan, which replaces an earlier plan. Its annotations take precedence over explicit. The query fails with ForeignTerm for a term of another formula, and with InvalidPlan for an operand order of a term that is not a product.

Source

pub fn max_length(self, steps: usize) -> Options

Searches only the words of at most steps steps (lock-step tuples, padding included). The search then does not expand a macrostate reached in steps steps, and when it leaves one unexpanded the Outcome without a witness is bounded: no word of at most steps steps is in the relation, but longer ones may be. materialize ignores it.

The antichain then skips a macrostate only for one found in at most as many steps.

use mata_lazy::{Formula, Options};
use mata_lazy::regex::Regex;

let mut formula = Formula::new();
let long = formula.leaf(&Regex::new("aaaa")?)?;
let outcome = formula.check(long, &Options::new().max_length(3))?;
assert!(outcome.witness.is_none() && outcome.bounded && !outcome.is_empty());
assert!(formula.check(long, &Options::new().max_length(4))?.witness.is_some());
Source

pub fn shortest(self, enabled: bool) -> Options

Searches breadth first (off by default), so that the witness has the fewest steps of any word in the relation. The search then numbers every initial macrostate of the checked term before it expands one, and keeps the whole frontier, which can take more memory than the default depth-first search. The antichain skips a macrostate only for one found in at most as many steps. materialize ignores it.
