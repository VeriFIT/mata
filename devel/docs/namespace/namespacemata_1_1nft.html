enum class CompositionNormalization : std::uint8_t

How compose() normalizes its result after the construction.

Every mode keeps the relation and the level of every state; state numbering is not part of the contract, and neither is the placement of EPSILON, which are_equivalent() reads as a letter.

Values:

enumerator None

Return the result as constructed (trimmed when the synchronization levels are projected out). Rewrite only the states the fast composer generated itself (EPSILON padding, bridges over a replaced synchronization, and waiting states): close the whole-round EPSILON runs through generated states with exactly one incoming and one outgoing transition, outside EPSILON cycles and where the result does not grow, then merge generated states of the same level with the same outgoing transitions. Near-linear in the generated states and their transitions. The general composition generates no such states and is left as constructed.

enumerator GeneratedOnly
enumerator Full

GeneratedOnly, then the generic remove_epsilon() (with JumpMode::NoJump only, as it reads every transition as one level long), Nft::trim() and simulation-based reduce(). Far more expensive than the composition itself; only for callers who want the smallest result.

enumerator Auto

None: GeneratedOnly costs more in the composition than it saves in a following composition on the transducers of string constraints, whose results it shrinks by a few percent.

enumerator Trim

None, then trimmed, also when the synchronization levels are kept. The fast composer then builds the result forward and from the final states (by composing the reverted operands) in turns with a growing state budget and keeps whichever finishes first: on the transducers of string constraints, either direction can build tens of times more states that the trim removes than the other. Under None and Auto it builds forward only, which is cheaper when the forward direction wins, as it does on most compositions. Operands it reads through the cursors of their jump transitions are composed forward only.

Nft compose(const Nft &lhs, const Nft &rhs, const utils::OrdVector<Level> &lhs_sync_levels, const utils::OrdVector<Level> &rhs_sync_levels, bool project_out_sync_levels = true, JumpMode jump_mode = JumpMode::RepeatSymbol, CompositionMode composition_mode = CompositionMode::Auto, CompositionNormalization normalization = CompositionNormalization::Auto)

Composes two NFTs.

This function computes the composition of two NFTs, lhs and rhs, by aligning their synchronization levels. Vectors of synchronization levels must be non-empty and of the same size. The levels of the resulting NFT are formed, iteratively, from the non-synchronizing levels of lhs, followed by the non-synchronizing levels of rhs, followed by the next synchronizing level (if not projected out by setting project_out_sync_levels to true). For example, given lhs with 7 levels, rhs with 8 levels, lhs_sync_levels=<0,2,5>, rhs_sync_levels=<1,4,5>, the resulting NFT levels will correspond to (assuming that we are not projecting out the synchronized levels)

  • rhs level 0

  • synchronized level from level 0 of lhs and level 1 of rhs

  • lhs level 1

  • rhs levels 2 and 3

  • synchronized level from level 2 of lhs and level 4 of rhs

  • lhs levels 3 and 4

  • synchronized level from level 5 of lhs and level 5 of rhs

  • lhs level 6

  • rhs levels 6 and 7

Parameters:

  • lhs – [in] First transducer to compose.

  • rhs – [in] Second transducer to compose.

  • lhs_sync_levels – [in] Ordered vector of synchronization levels of the lhs.

  • rhs_sync_levels – [in] Ordered vector of synchronization levels of the rhs.

  • project_out_sync_levels – [in] Whether we want to project out the synchronization levels.

  • jump_mode – [in] Specifies if the symbol on a jump transition (a transition with a length greater than 1) is interpreted as a sequence repeating the same symbol or as a single instance of the symbol followed by a sequence of DONT_CARE.

  • composition_mode – [in] Mode of composition to use. Default is Auto, which takes FastNoJump for one synchronization level per NFT when jump_mode is NoJump, or when neither NFT has a jump transition, EPSILON or DONT_CARE (the jump mode then does not matter), and General otherwise. General mode supports all NFTs, while FastNoJump mode is optimized for NFTs without jump transitions and with only one synchronization level.

  • normalization – [in] How to normalize the result after the construction; see CompositionNormalization.

Throws:

std::invalid_argument – When the request violates the precondition above, when composition_mode is FastNoJump and jump_mode is not NoJump, or when FastNoJump is asked for more than one synchronization level.

Pre:

The synchronization level vectors are non-empty, of equal size and strictly increasing, every level is below its operand’s levels.num_of_levels, and the composition leaves at least one level; otherwise std::invalid_argument naming the violated condition is thrown, in Release builds too.

Returns:

A new NFT after the composition.

Nft invert_levels(const Nft &aut, JumpMode jump_mode = JumpMode::RepeatSymbol)

Inverts the levels of the given transducer aut.

The function inverts the levels of the transducer, i.e., the level 0 becomes the last level, level 1 becomes the second last level, and so on.

The segment between two level-zero states is read backwards, so an inner state is split by the context (state, head, tail) it is used in: one inverted state per inner state that lies on some head -> tail path. Sharing an inverted state across contexts would add tuples the original does not have (see the comment at the construction), sharing inside one context cannot. The result therefore grows with the input and the number of contexts, not with the number of paths.

Parameters:

  • aut – [in] The transducer for inverting levels. Must be layered: every transition out of a state with a nonzero level reaches a strictly higher level or level 0, and every level is below levels.num_of_levels.

  • jump_mode – [in] Specifies if the symbol on a jump transition (a transition with a length greater than 1) is interpreted as a sequence repeating the same symbol or as a single instance of the symbol followed by a sequence of DONT_CARE symbols.

Throws:

std::invalid_argument – If aut is not layered, i.e., if some segment between two level-zero states contains a cycle or a transition that does not increase the level.

Returns:

A new transducer with inverted levels.

Nft remove_epsilon(const Nft &aut, Symbol epsilon = EPSILON)

Remove simple epsilon transitions.

Simple epsilon transitions are the transitions of the form q0 -epsilon-> q1 -epsilon-> q2 -epsilon-> … -epsilon-> qn where q0 and qn are level 0 states, the states in-between are states with level 1, 2, …, num_of_levels and for each qi, for 0 < i < n, there is only 1 transition going to qi (the transition qi-1 -epsilon-> qi) and only 1 transition going from qi (the transition qi -epsilon -> qi+1). This means that if there was some state p0 going with epsilon to q1, these to epsilon transitions would not be removed.

Furthermore, this assumes that the NFT aut does not have jump transitions.

The resulting automaton has the same number of states as aut, just the transitions can change. It is recommended to run trim() after this function.

Parameters:

  • aut – NFT without jump transitions

  • epsilon – symbol representing epsilon

Returns:

NFT whose language is same as aut but does not contain simple epsilon transitions

void add_transition(State source, const std::string &symbol_name, State target, Alphabet *alphabet = nullptr)

Add a new transition from source to target labeled by the symbol named symbol_name.

Convenience wrapper mirroring nfa::Nfa::add_transition(), translating symbol_name to a Symbol via the resolved alphabet instead of requiring the caller to translate it themselves.

Unlike the plain-Symbol overloads above, symbol_name is translated separately for each level the transition spans (i.e., levels[source], levels[next_level_after(levels[source])], …, up to but excluding levels[target]), each via the alphabet resolved for that level (see resolve_alphabet): alphabet, when non-null, takes precedence over this->alphabets (used in AlphabetLevels::Mode ::MultiLevel via the level being translated for; ignored in Global mode) over this->alphabet.

If every affected level translates symbol_name to the same Symbol, a single jump transition from source directly to target is added (no new states). Otherwise, since a single jump transition cannot carry a different symbol per level, the jump is unwound into a sequence of ordinary, single-level transitions, each carrying its own level’s translation of symbol_name, through freshly created inner states.

  • symbol_name – [in] Name of the symbol labeling the new transition, translated separately for each level the transition spans.

  • target – [in] Target state of the new transition. Must already exist.

  • alphabet – [in] Alphabet to translate symbol_name with on every affected level, taking precedence over this->alphabets/this->alphabet when non-null.

Throws:

std::runtime_error – If no alphabet is available to translate symbol_name for some affected level.

Nft &insert_identity(State state, const std::vector<Symbol> &symbols, JumpMode jump_mode = JumpMode::RepeatSymbol)

Inserts identity transitions into the NFT.

For each symbol, the result reads the symbol on every level and returns to state, so the inserted loops accept exactly the tuples (w, w, …, w). The representation depends on jump_mode:

  • JumpMode::RepeatSymbol: one jump transition state -symbol-> state spanning all levels, which repeats the symbol on every level. Read the result with JumpMode::RepeatSymbol, not with JumpMode::NoJump or by the fast composition (CompositionMode::FastNoJump), which require jump-free NFTs.

  • JumpMode::NoJump and JumpMode::AppendDontCares: an explicit loop of num_of_levels single-level transitions through fresh inner states. A jump under AppendDontCares would pad the symbol with DONT_CARE, which is no identity.

A one-level NFT gets a single self-loop in every mode. A state on a nonzero level always gets the explicit loop, as a jump must start and end on level 0.

  • symbols – The vector of symbols used for the identity transition. Identity will be created for each symbol in the vector.

  • jump_mode – How to represent the identity, see above.

Self with inserted identity.

Nft compose_general(const Nft &lhs, const Nft &rhs, const utils::OrdVector<Level> &lhs_sync_levels, const utils::OrdVector<Level> &rhs_sync_levels, bool project_out_sync_levels = true, JumpMode jump_mode = JumpMode::RepeatSymbol, CompositionNormalization normalization = CompositionNormalization::Auto)

Composes two NFTs.

This function computes the composition of two NFTs, lhs and rhs, by aligning their synchronization levels. Vectors of synchronization levels must be non-empty and of the same size. The levels of the resulting NFT are formed, iteratively, from the non-synchronizing levels of lhs, followed by the non-synchronizing levels of rhs, followed by the next synchronizing level (if not projected out by setting project_out_sync_levels to true). For example, given lhs with 7 levels, rhs with 8 levels, lhs_sync_levels=<0,2,5>, rhs_sync_levels=<1,4,5>, the resulting NFT levels will correspond to (assuming that we are not projecting out the synchronized levels)

  • rhs level 0

  • synchronized level from level 0 of lhs and level 1 of rhs

  • lhs level 1

  • rhs levels 2 and 3

  • synchronized level from level 2 of lhs and level 4 of rhs

  • lhs levels 3 and 4

  • synchronized level from level 5 of lhs and level 5 of rhs

  • lhs level 6

  • rhs levels 6 and 7

NOTE: If you have only a single synchronization level per NFT, consider compose_fast() for better performance; it reads jump transitions as well.

Operands whose transitions all go to a later level of their round or end it are composed by compose_general_direct() (with more than one aligned level), all others by the pipeline of compose_general_reference(); both accept the same relation.

  • rhs – [in] Second transducer to compose.

  • lhs_sync_levels – [in] Ordered vector of synchronization levels of the lhs.

  • rhs_sync_levels – [in] Ordered vector of synchronization levels of the rhs.

  • project_out_sync_levels – [in] Whether we want to project out the synchronization levels.

  • jump_mode – [in] Specifies if the symbol on a jump transition (a transition with a length greater than 1) is interpreted as a sequence repeating the same symbol or as a single instance of the symbol followed by a sequence of DONT_CARE.

  • normalization – [in] Only CompositionNormalization::Full and Trim change the result: the general composition generates no states of its own for GeneratedOnly to rewrite.

Nft compose_fast(const Nft &lhs, const Nft &rhs, Level lhs_sync_level, Level rhs_sync_level, bool project_out_sync_levels, JumpMode jump_mode, CompositionNormalization normalization = CompositionNormalization::Auto)

Composes two NFTs with a single synchronization level, reading their jump transitions in jump_mode.

The levels of the resulting NFT are in the following order:

  • levels of lhs before its synchronization level

  • levels of rhs before its synchronization level

  • the synchronizing level (possibly missing if project_out_sync_levels is true)

  • levels of lhs after its synchronization level

  • levels of rhs after its synchronization level

A jump transition is read lazily, one level at a time: the jumps of a symbol post share a cursor per level they cover after their first one, which reads the symbol of the jump under JumpMode::RepeatSymbol and DONT_CARE under JumpMode::AppendDontCares, and a configuration of the composition pairs such cursors as it pairs states. A level inside a jump thus synchronizes on the symbol the jump reads there. The operands are not copied, and DONT_CARE is not spelled out. Under JumpMode::RepeatSymbol, an EPSILON transition between two zero-level states is a whole round of EPSILON, as under JumpMode::NoJump.

The result has no jump transitions: a jump is unwound into one transition per level, through the result states of the configurations with its cursors, so it reads the same in every jump mode. Its relation is the one of compose_fast_no_jump() on the operands with their jumps unwound in jump_mode (DONT_CARE kept), which is the relation of compose_general() whenever the operands have no EPSILON or DONT_CARE, no jump of jump_mode JumpMode::AppendDontCares reads DONT_CARE on a synchronization level, and, under JumpMode::RepeatSymbol, neither operand with a jump is composed with a one-level operand. The two compositions place EPSILON differently, and compose_general() matches DONT_CARE with EPSILON (#561) and lets an operand composed with a one-level operand choose its jump again inside it.

Parameters:

  • lhs – [in] First transducer to compose.

  • rhs – [in] Second transducer to compose.

  • lhs_sync_level – [in] The synchronization level of the lhs.

  • rhs_sync_level – [in] The synchronization level of the rhs.

  • project_out_sync_levels – [in] Whether we want to project out the synchronization levels.

  • jump_mode – [in] How a jump transition reads the levels after its first one. With JumpMode::NoJump, the operands must not have jump transitions other than fast EPSILON transitions, as in compose_fast_no_jump().

  • normalization – [in] How to normalize the result after the construction; see CompositionNormalization.

Throws:

std::invalid_argument – When a synchronization level is out of range for its operand, when the composition would leave no levels, or, under a jump mode other than JumpMode::NoJump, when a transition goes to an earlier level of its round. The no-jump shape of the operands under JumpMode::NoJump is only checked by MATA_ASSERT.

Returns:

A new NFT after the composition.

struct CompositionSchedule
#include <algorithms.hh>

The common level layout of the general composition of two NFTs.

The aligned levels come in blocks, one per synchronization level and one after the last: the levels only lhs reads, then the levels only rhs reads, then the synchronization level, which the last block lacks. The result of the composition has the aligned levels in this order, without the synchronization levels when they are projected out. Example:

Nft construct(const parser::ParsedSection &parsec, Alphabet *alphabet, NameStateMap *state_map = nullptr)

Loads an automaton from a single parsed section of the mata format.

Unlike the IntermediateAut overload below, this one reads parsec verbatim: Initial and Final are taken as plain lists of state names and a body line is always a source symbol target triple. State names are therefore arbitrary strings, not subject to the naming markers that IntermediateAut parses; symbol names are still resolved through alphabet, which decides which of them are valid. This overload is also the only one that reads the level metadata: Levels assigns state:level pairs and LevelsNum sets the number of levels, both of which the IntermediateAut path ignores. The two overloads consequently accept different inputs and cannot be merged without changing observable behavior.

Parameters:

The names it maps beforehand keep their states, each allocated (with the default level) when the input uses its name; a final formula of an IntermediateAut that is true or negates states uses every name of the map it does not negate. Every other name gets the lowest state that the automaton does not have yet and that no name of the map had beforehand, so that it never shares the state of a mapped name, used later or not at all.

It assumes that nfa is a representation of an NFT without jump transitions. It assigns to each state the level based on the distance from the initial state. For example, if there are 2 levels, the initial states are level 0, the successor states are level 1, the states after that level 0, etc.
