Namespace mata::applications::strings::seg_nfa¶

namespace seg_nfa

Segment Automata including structs and algorithms.

These are automata whose state space can be split into several segments connected by ε-transitions in a chain. No other ε-transitions are allowed. As a consequence, no ε-transitions can appear in a cycle. Segment automaton can have initial states only in the first segment and final states only in the last segment.

void segs_one_initial_final(const std::vector<Nfa> &segments, bool include_empty, const State &unused_state, std::map<std::pair<State, State>, NoodleSegment> &out)

segs_one_initial_final

segments_one_initial_final[init, final] is the segment of one of the segments such that init and final are one of the initial and final states of the segment, i.e. the language from init to final in the segment; also segments_one_initial_final[unused_state, final] is used for the first segment (where we always want all initial states, only final state changes) and segments_one_initial_final[init, unused_state] is similarly for the last segment. All pairs of one final state share one automaton (see NoodleSegment); the first segment, whose pairs differ in the final state only, gets a single initial state by unifying its initial states. The map is ordered so that all pairs of one initial state are contiguous: noodlify_mult_eps() visits them with one range scan instead of probing every final state.

std::vector<Noodle> noodlify_for_equation(const std::vector<std::reference_wrapper<Nfa>> &lhs_automata, const Nfa &rhs_automaton, bool include_empty = false, const ParameterMap &params = {{"reduce", "false"}})

Create noodles for left and right side of equation.

Segment automaton is a chain of finite automata (segments) connected via ε-transitions. A noodle is a copy of the segment automaton with exactly one ε-transition between each two consecutive segments.

Mata cannot work with equations, queries etc. Hence, we compute the noodles for the equation, but represent the equation in a way that libMata understands. The left side automata represent the left side of the equation and the right automaton represents the right side of the equation. To create noodles, we need a segment automaton representing the intersection. That can be achieved by computing a product of both sides. First, the left side has to be concatenated over an epsilon transitions into a single automaton to compute the intersection on, though.

Parameters:

  • lhs_automata – [in] Sequence of segment automata for left side of an equation to noodlify.

  • rhs_automaton – [in] Segment automaton for right side of an equation to noodlify.

  • include_empty – [in] Whether to also include empty noodles.

  • params – [in] Additional parameters for the noodlification:

    • ”reduce”: “false”, “forward”, “backward”, “bidirectional”; Execute forward, backward or bidirectional simulation minimization before noodlification.

Returns:

A list of all (non-empty) noodles.

struct NoodleSegment
#include <strings.hh>

A segment of a noodle: the part of a segment automaton between one of its initial states and one of its final states.

automaton is the reduced part of the segment automaton that reaches the final state, shared by all segments that end in that final state; the segment is the language of initial in it. The initial states of automaton itself are hence not those of the segment. The parts of one final state overlap almost completely, so sharing them keeps one automaton per final state instead of one per pair of an initial and a final state.

struct NoodleOrder
#include <strings.hh>

The order in which the noodlify_*_lazy() functions emit the noodles.

The search is depth-first over the segments; at every node it orders the candidates for the next segment (the pairs of an initial and a final state of it). The order is greedy: a candidate ranked first leads to the next noodle, not necessarily to the best noodle overall.
